This is a product-risk story, not a product-death story. Cursor still sells an editor. Anthropic still sells Claude. The question for a solo operator is whether GPT in the picker is load-bearing for how you ship.

We are writing this as MorningStacks, for operators who pay their own bills. Dipen has not confirmed any first-person test of the picker, of Auto mode, or of a repo migration. Those lines stay out.

## What did OpenAI actually announce, and when is the proposed shutoff?

OpenAI's own post, dated 28 August 2026, is the source of record: [Our decision on Cursor following its acquisition by SpaceX](https://openai.com/index/our-decision-on-cursor-following-its-acquisition-by-spacex/). WebFetch timed out on that URL this pass. Curl returned HTTP 200 on 2 September 2026. The quotes below come from that curl text.

The first paragraph carries the whole operational fact:

> Today, we notified SpaceX that we intend to wind down our contract providing OpenAI models to Cursor, with a proposed shutoff date of November 12, 2026. To maximize the time that developers can retain access to our models through Cursor, we are giving the maximum notice provided by our contract.

Three details sit inside that quote.

1. The notice went to SpaceX, not to a public Cursor blog we could fetch.
2. The date is a **proposed** shutoff. OpenAI wrote it that way.
3. OpenAI frames the window as the maximum notice in the contract.

From 28 August to 12 November is about ten weeks and a few days. That is enough time to dual-home a repo. It is not enough time if you wait for a press cycle to resolve the corporate fight.

Later in the same post, OpenAI says an upcoming model named Astra will not be provided to Cursor, that it will support developers in the transition, and that the custom agreement allows cancellation after a change of control. It also writes that it is holding cancellation to the latest date it can while not providing future models to Cursor.

That is OpenAI's position, and we are quoting it as OpenAI's position.

A Cursor.com official response to the 28 August post was not fetched. Cursor's blog does have [Cursor is now a part of SpaceX](https://cursor.com/blog/joining-spacex) (14 August 2026), which is the acquisition announcement, not an answer to OpenAI. We did not invent a SpaceX reply or a legal letter. If Cursor publishes a reply after this draft, add that URL in an update.

## Why does OpenAI say it is cancelling after the SpaceX acquisition?

OpenAI's stated reason is terms-of-service confidence, not a product review of Cursor.

From the same 28 August 2026 post:

> We are making this choice because we cannot be confident that SpaceX will use our technology within our terms of service, based on our experience with Elon Musk's companies violating contracts.

The post then cites OpenAI's history with Twitter after Musk acquired it, and an admission under oath, as OpenAI describes it, that xAI, now also part of SpaceX, had violated OpenAI's terms.

We are not litigating that history here. For a solo operator, the useful part is narrower: OpenAI is treating Cursor as a SpaceX-controlled partner, and it is using a change-of-control clause. The fight is corporate. Your repo is not a party to it, but your model picker is.

Do not treat social recaps as the announcement. Do not treat Reuters as a source for this piece; that page returned 401 in research. The OpenAI URL above is enough.

## Does this cancel Cursor the product, or only OpenAI models inside Cursor?

Only the OpenAI models inside Cursor, as far as OpenAI's post goes. The post is about winding down "our contract providing OpenAI models to Cursor." It is not a notice that Cursor the IDE shuts down.

That still matters if your daily path is GPT-5.6 Sol, Terra, or Luna in Agent. It matters less if you already live on Claude, Composer, or Grok.

OpenAI did not name which GPT IDs go dark first. What we could not confirm on 2 September 2026:

- Exact GPT model IDs that go dark versus stay.
- Whether Composer remains fully usable with no OpenAI models.
- Whether Grok access on Cursor pricing is independent of this contract.
- Whether existing Cursor Pro usage pools still include OpenAI after 12 November.
- Whether Auto mode already avoids OpenAI models. Dipen has not confirmed what Auto does in his picker.

Cursor staff did confirm something adjacent on 1 September 2026. In [Claude Fable 5.1 - Out Now](https://forum.cursor.com/t/claude-fable-5-1-out-now/170246), Colin (Cursor staff) wrote that Fable 5.1 is in the product, that it scored 73.4% on CursorBench 3.2 at max effort, and that Privacy Mode users must approve Fable 5.1 data retention in the Dashboard. A community question on 2 September 2026 asked whether Fable is chosen in Auto or only manually. It was unanswered at fetch. Do not assume Auto will rescue you.

Anthropic's launch post for [Claude Fable 5.1 and Mythos 5.1](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-and-mythos-5-1) (fetched 2 September 2026) lists CursorBench 3.2.0 scores: Fable 5.1 at 73.4%, Fable 5 at 70.5%, Opus 5 at 70.0%, GPT-5.6 Sol at 67.2%. Those are vendor bench numbers. They are not a MorningStacks bake-off.

Mythos 5.1, in the same post, is the same weights with looser cyber and life-science safeguards, limited to vetted US programs. It is not a solo-operator default.
