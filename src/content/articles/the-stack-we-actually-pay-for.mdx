Software bills are quietly the second-largest line item in most early-stage operating budgets, behind payroll. We know this because we audited our own.

Forty-three SaaS tools sit in our shared password manager. Twelve of them are pulling more than their weight. The other thirty-one are running on inertia — paid for in a quarter we don't remember, kept around because cancelling feels like admitting a mistake.

This is the editorial inversion we're built around: we'd rather tell you which twelve, than write a 5,000-word breakdown of all forty-three.

<PullQuote cite="The MorningStacks editors">
The right software stack is not the most software you can afford. It's the smallest stack that lets a small team do work a big team would have needed last year.
</PullQuote>

## The shortlist

Three tools made the cut for almost everyone we talked to. Two of them you've heard of. One of them you've probably underused.

<ProductCard product="linear" />

If you ship software and you're still on Jira, Linear is not a "nice to have" — it's a velocity tax you're paying every day. We've moved three teams onto it in the last six months. None of them want to go back.

<ProductCard product="raycast" />

The most under-rated install on this list. Treat the first week as a learning week — you will feel slower before you feel faster — and by week three you will be reaching for the keyboard before the mouse.

## The middle tier

Tools that earn their keep without quite earning religious devotion.

<ProductCard product="notion" />

Notion does too many things, and that is both its strength and its tax. Use it for the wiki and the specs. Don't try to run your whole operating system in it. Past thirty people, you'll need a real project tracker — see above.

## What we'd cut today

We are not going to publish a list of names here, because we'd rather review them properly first. But the pattern is clear: tools that bill annually, are used twice a quarter, and were bought for a project that ended. Audit your own list. The exercise pays for itself.
