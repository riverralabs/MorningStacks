If you're a solo operator, indie founder, or creator picking a newsletter platform today, you're staring at three names: **Beehiiv**, **Substack**, and **Kit** (formerly ConvertKit). Every other "best newsletter platform 2026" article ranks them by feature count or affiliate priority. This one doesn't.

Three things changed in the last 18 months that make this decision harder than it used to be:

- **Substack** raised $100M at a $1.1B valuation in July 2025 (BOND, Chernin Group, a16z), and despite the war chest, kept the 10% revenue share. That cut now feels permanent, not transitional.
- **Beehiiv** raised $33M Series B (NEA) in April 2024, hit roughly $30M ARR by mid-2025, and shipped a Winter 2025 release that turned it from "newsletter tool" into "creator OS" (AI website builder, podcast hosting, digital products at 0% commission).
- **Kit** rebranded from ConvertKit on October 1, 2024, expanded its free tier to 10,000 subscribers, then raised paid prices ~34% in September 2025. Creators who'd been on legacy ConvertKit pricing for years suddenly faced a real bill.

The 2026 newsletter founder isn't choosing between "email tool" and "publishing platform." They're choosing between three different theories of how creators make money on the internet. Beehiiv thinks it's ads + paid subs. Substack thinks it's the network. Kit thinks it's the product catalog behind the email.

## Discovery and network effects: the underrated decision driver

This is what most comparison posts miss. Where will new subscribers come from? Each platform has a different answer.

### Substack: Notes + Recommendations

This is the strongest pure organic-growth engine in the space. At Substack's NYC Notes Night (2025), co-founder Hamish McKenzie and ML Head Mike Cohen disclosed that the Notes feed sourced over **32 million new free subscriptions and 500,000 new paid subscriptions** in a single three-month window in 2025.

You cannot replicate that on Beehiiv or Kit at zero marketing budget. If you have no audience, no paid acquisition budget, and your product is the writing itself, this number is the entire argument for Substack.

### Beehiiv Boosts

A paid acquisition marketplace. You bid $1–$5 per qualified subscriber. Pay only on conversion. Beehiiv takes 20% of GMV. Many Beehiiv operators describe Boosts as "free money" when used as a recommender (earning side), not a buyer.

Boosts is more transactional than Substack's network. Less serendipity, more ROI clarity.

### Kit Creator Network + Sponsor Network

- **Creator Network:** Free cross-promotion + SparkLoop-powered Paid Recommendations ($1–$4+ per sub). Kit takes 23.5% blended.
- **Sponsor Network:** Brand ads in your sends, Kit handles relationships. Requires 10K+ subs and weekly cadence.

Kit's network is the smallest of the three but the most relevant if you're already inside the Kit ecosystem of course creators.

## Personal experience: what I actually ran into

I'll be transparent: I'm running MorningStacks's email list on Beehiiv, and the decision wasn't ideological. It was math. After comparing all three on a real spreadsheet (the same one above), Beehiiv came out cheapest at every paid-revenue tier above $1K/mo, and the partner program was the most lucrative for the kind of editorial-affiliate publication I'm building.

The friction was real, though. The $0 → $49/mo cliff at 2,500 subs is psychologically brutal. It lands the same week you're celebrating crossing 2K, and you're suddenly paying $49/mo before any monetization is firing. I almost moved to Kit's free 10K plan to defer the cost.

The thing that kept me on Beehiiv was integration depth: I had n8n workflows, Zapier triggers, and a custom domain set up in under 30 minutes, which on Substack would have required scrapers and on Kit would have meant rebuilding a website I didn't want to maintain. The AI writing tools also genuinely saved time on subject line variations. Not transformational, but enough that I noticed when I tested Kit's lighter AI for a week and missed Beehiiv's.

If I were starting over today as a pure writer with no business plan, I'd probably try Substack first for the Notes flywheel, accept the 10% as a marketing tax, and migrate once paid revenue justified the move. Your mileage will vary entirely based on what you're already running.

## The decision framework

Stop comparing features. Answer these four questions in order.

### 1. Will you turn on paid subscriptions in the next 12 months?

- **Yes** → Skip Substack unless you specifically need Notes discovery. The 10% cut compounds.
- **No, never** → Substack is fine. You're paying $0 forever.

### 2. Do you sell digital products, courses, or coaching?

- **Yes, primarily** → Kit. The product UX and tag-based automations pay for themselves on a single launch.
- **No, just newsletter** → Beehiiv or Substack.

### 3. What's your primary growth channel today?

- **Twitter / LinkedIn / SEO / owned audience** → Beehiiv or Kit (you don't need Substack's network).
- **Nothing yet, no marketing budget** → Substack (you need Notes more than you need features).

### 4. How long do you plan to use this platform?

- **3+ years** → Pick the one whose pricing trajectory you can stomach. Kit raised prices recently. Substack hasn't dropped its 10%. Beehiiv's $49 entry has held since 2023.
- **Just experimenting** → Start free on Kit (10K subs) or Beehiiv (2.5K subs). Avoid Substack lock-in.
