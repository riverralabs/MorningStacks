There is a faster way to do this.

We could spin up a free trial, run through the onboarding, take some screenshots, copy the marketing site's feature list into our own words, slap a 4.2 rating on it, and publish before lunch. That is, broadly speaking, how the affiliate review industry works.

We are not going to do that.

## What "tested" means here

When we say we tested a piece of software, we mean one of two things, both verifiable:

1. **We used it in production.** Real team, real workflow, at least one full pricing cycle. Linear was tested across three teams and twelve sprints. Notion was tested across two writing teams over six months. Raycast has been on the daily driver of every editor here for eighteen months.

2. **We ran a structured trial.** When the tool is too expensive or too new for option 1, we do a 30-day structured trial against a checklist we publish, on a representative use case, with at least two team members involved. The methodology page documents exactly what that looks like.

Either way, the date you see at the bottom of every review (`Last tested: month, year`) is real. If it's more than nine months old, we re-test.

<PullQuote>
The job of a review is not to summarize the marketing page. The job is to tell you what the marketing page is hiding.
</PullQuote>

## What we won't do for money

The disclosure page lists this in legal language, but here it is in plain English:

- We don't accept money to review a tool.
- We don't accept money to give a tool a higher score.
- We don't accept money to keep a tool in a roundup.
- We don't accept money to remove a competitor from a roundup.
- We accept affiliate commissions, which are paid by the vendor when a reader clicks through and buys. The commission is the same whether we score the tool a 5.0 or a 1.0.

The economic model is straightforward: we get paid more when we are right, because readers come back. We get paid less when we are wrong. That is not a perfect alignment of incentives, but it is a real one — and it is the closest a publication of this size can get to honest.

## What this costs us

A 30-day test costs us roughly the same labor as a 30-minute one would cost a competitor. We publish fewer pieces. We make less per piece. We are slower to react to product launches.

We think this is the correct trade. You can decide that for yourself.
