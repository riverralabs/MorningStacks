A launcher is software you use thirty times a day. Picking the right one compounds.

We tested all five major options on macOS over four months on three different machines. Below, in order, is what we'd install on a fresh laptop today.

## 1. Raycast — our pick

<ProductCard product="raycast" />

Raycast wins on three axes: speed, ecosystem, and the depth of what you can do without leaving the launcher. It's the only one of these tools we recommend buying Pro for, because the AI features are well-integrated rather than bolted on. If you do nothing else from this list, install Raycast.

## 2. Alfred — the venerable second

If you've used Alfred for a decade and your workflows are tuned, there is no urgent reason to switch. The Powerpack is a one-time purchase, the Workflows ecosystem is mature, and the app is rock solid. The reason it's not our pick is that the modern feel — extensions, AI, snippets, clipboard — has all moved to Raycast. Alfred is the dependable choice; Raycast is the one moving forward.

## 3. LaunchBar — the keyboardist's keyboardist

LaunchBar's instant-send paradigm (select a thing, hit a hotkey, do something with it) is genuinely faster than any competitor for some workflows. If you grew up on Quicksilver, this is your tool. The aesthetic is dated and the extension ecosystem is small. We respect it. We don't recommend it.

## 4. Spotlight — the default that's quietly fine

Apple's built-in launcher has gotten meaningfully better in the last two macOS releases. For users who only need to launch apps, find files, and do quick math, Spotlight is now sufficient. Anything beyond that — clipboard history, snippets, custom workflows — and you need to upgrade.

## 5. The new wave (Sol, Bunch, Maestro AI)

We tested three AI-first launchers in this category. None of them are ready. The pitch — type natural language, get a result — is compelling, but the execution lags Raycast Pro on every dimension we care about. We'll re-test in six months.

## Methodology

We installed each launcher on a 2024 MacBook Pro and a 2026 Mac mini, running the same set of tasks daily for at least 30 days each: app launching, file search, clipboard, snippet expansion, window management, custom commands. We measured time-to-result for ten common tasks and tracked breakage. Full notes are on the [methodology](/methodology/) page.

Install Raycast. Pay for Pro after a week if you find yourself reaching for it more than ten times a day. You will.
