Notion vs Obsidian is less of a head-to-head than it looks. They solve different problems, and once you know which problem you have, the choice usually makes itself.

Obsidian is a Markdown editor for files on your own device. Notion is a cloud workspace for docs, databases, and teams. We compared both on **8 October 2026** using their official pricing and help pages.

## Notion vs Obsidian at a glance

<div class="table-wrap">

| | Obsidian | Notion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where notes live | Markdown files on your device | Notion's cloud |
| Free plan | Full app, no sign-up | Unlimited blocks for individuals |
| Cheapest paid option | Sync $4/user/mo (annual) | Plus $10/member/mo (annual) |
| Offline | Everything, always | Selected pages (Free), recents and favorites (paid) |

</div>

Plugin count is from Obsidian's public community-plugins list on GitHub, counted 8 Oct 2026. Prices are USD.

## How do Notion and Obsidian work differently?

**Obsidian** opens a folder of plain Markdown files, called a vault, and gives you an editor with links, backlinks, a graph view, and plugins. Your notes stay on your device. Obsidian's pricing page says its apps don't collect telemetry, and that Sync is end-to-end encrypted so Obsidian can't read synced notes either.

**Notion** is a hosted workspace. Pages, databases, wikis, and project boards live on Notion's servers, and you open them in the browser or the apps. Everything is shared by default within a workspace, which is the point.

That one difference drives most of the trade-offs below.

For one person, Obsidian with Sync Standard is $48 a year against $120 for Notion Plus. For a 5-person team, it's $240 for Obsidian Sync against $600 for Notion Plus or $1,200 for Business.

Both free tiers are usable long term. Notion Free is generous for one person; Obsidian free is the full app.

## Which is better for personal notes and writing?

**Obsidian.** For a single person's notes, research, journaling, or drafts, it's the stronger tool:

- **Speed and ownership.** Notes are local Markdown files. You can open them in any editor, back them up however you like, and keep them if Obsidian ever disappears.
- **Linking.** Backlinks and the graph view are built for connecting ideas across hundreds or thousands of notes.
- **Offline by default.** Planes, trains, and bad Wi-Fi don't matter.
- **Cost.** Free, or $48 a year with Sync Standard.

Notion works fine for personal notes too, especially if you like database views for reading lists or habit trackers. The cost is that your notes live in Notion's cloud and offline access is partial.

## Which is better for teams?

**Notion**, for most teams. It's built for shared work:

- Shared pages with permissions and guests (10 guests on Free, unlimited on paid plans).
- Databases with relations, filters, charts, forms, and automations.
- Web publishing with free notion.site pages, plus SEO controls on paid plans.
- On Business, SAML SSO, private teamspaces, and Notion Agent for AI work across your docs and connected apps.

Obsidian does support teams. Sync can share vaults, and notes update in real time across collaborators. Sync is priced per user, though, and permissions and databases are far lighter than Notion's. It works best for small groups who already like working in Markdown, such as engineers or writers.

If your team also needs an issue tracker, our [Linear vs Jira](/productivity/linear-vs-jira-small-saas/) comparison covers that side. Many small teams use Notion for docs and Linear for engineering work.

## How do offline access and privacy compare?

**Obsidian** keeps everything on your device. Its pricing page says the apps don't collect telemetry, it never sells user data, and Sync data is end-to-end encrypted so Obsidian can't read it.

**Notion** stores your workspace in its cloud. Offline access on Free means choosing specific pages to download. Paid plans automatically download recents and favorites. For Notion AI, Notion's pricing page lists 30-day data retention with its AI model providers on Free, Plus, and Business, and zero data retention on Enterprise.

If you keep sensitive notes, like client details or health records, Obsidian's local-first model is easier to reason about.

## What about AI features?

**Notion** has AI built in. Business and Enterprise include Notion Agent (chat, generate, autofill, translate), AI Meeting Notes, Enterprise Search across connected apps, and a research mode. Free and Plus get a limited trial.

**Obsidian** has no built-in AI assistant listed on its pricing or Sync pages. Community plugins connect it to AI models, which also means you pick the model and the plugin's privacy terms.

If AI drafting is central to your work, you may get more from a general assistant. Our [Perplexity Pro vs ChatGPT for research](/ai-tools/perplexity-pro-vs-chatgpt-research/) piece compares two common choices.

## Can you switch from Notion to Obsidian?

Yes, and it's officially supported. Obsidian's [Importer plugin](https://obsidian.md/help/import/notion) gives you two routes:

1. **Connect your Notion account** with an API token. This keeps databases and formulas by converting them to Obsidian Bases.
2. **Import Notion's zip export.** No token or internet needed, but databases aren't preserved.

Going the other way is simpler in principle, since Obsidian notes are Markdown files and Notion can import Markdown. We haven't tested either migration on a large workspace.

| --- | --- | --- |
| A writer, researcher, or student taking personal notes | Obsidian | Local files, fast linking, free |
| A developer keeping docs next to code | Obsidian | Plain Markdown works with Git and your editor |
| A founder running a small team | Notion | Shared docs, databases, and guests in one place |
| A team that needs SSO and admin controls | Notion Business | SAML SSO, private teamspaces, 90-day history |
| Privacy-focused, with sensitive notes | Obsidian | Data stays on your device; Sync is end-to-end encrypted |
| Someone who wants built-in AI on your docs | Notion Business | Notion Agent and Enterprise Search |

</div>
