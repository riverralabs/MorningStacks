There is a moment, somewhere in the second week of using Linear, when you stop noticing the software.

The keyboard shortcuts have stuck. Triage takes four minutes instead of forty. The UI has receded into the background, leaving only the work. This is, in our experience, what good software feels like — and it is rarer in this category than it should be.

<ProductCard product="linear" />

## The case for

Three things make Linear worth the switching cost.

**Speed.** Every interaction is sub-100 milliseconds, which sounds like a vanity metric until you do it for a week. Then you go back to a competitor and it feels broken. We don't have a better way to describe this — it's the difference between software that responds to you and software that you wait for.

**Opinionated defaults.** Cycles, triage, projects: these are not configurable to death. There is one good way to do each, and Linear has chosen it. If you don't like that approach, this is not your tool. If you've been bitten by years of customizing Jira boards into uselessness, this is the tool.

**Insights that aren't theatre.** The reporting dashboards are useful. Cycle health, throughput, scope creep — these are things engineering managers need to know, and Linear surfaces them without requiring you to build a Looker dashboard.

## The case against

Two things gave us pause.

**Pricing climbs fast.** $10/user/month is reasonable. By the time you've added Plus features and integrations, you're at $14–18 per seat, and at fifty seats that's a real line item. Compare carefully against your current spend.

**The opinionatedness is real.** If your engineering team genuinely needs a workflow that doesn't fit Linear's model — because of a regulated industry, a complex release process, or a hybrid hardware/software org — you will hit walls. Test the workflow you actually use, not the one you wish you had.

## How we tested

Twelve sprints. Three teams. One a five-person startup, one a thirty-person Series B, one a dispersed open-source project with sixty contributors. We migrated existing issues, ran our normal cycle cadence, and tracked time spent on the tracker itself versus time spent on real work.

Across all three, the time-on-tracker fell. The smallest team saw a 22% reduction; the largest, 41%. We are aware those are vibes-grade measurements; we publish them anyway because the direction is clear and consistent.

## Verdict

If you ship software and you're using Jira, Asana, or a homegrown system, Linear is the recommendation. The switching cost is real but pays for itself within a quarter. The pricing is fair. The product is excellent. We renewed all three accounts.
